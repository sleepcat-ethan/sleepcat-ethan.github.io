LIN (Local Interconnect Network) is the low-speed, single-wire bus that fills the gap CAN leaves. Window lifts, mirrors, seats, wipers — they don't need bandwidth, and CAN is too expensive to wire to every one of them.

It tops out at 20 kbit/s over a single wire and 12 V supply, up to 40 m, with one master and up to 15 slaves.

The master always starts the conversation. It sends the header — a Break (at least 13 dominant bits), a Sync byte (0x55), and a PID (6-bit ID plus 2 parity bits) — then the addressed slave returns data and a one-byte checksum. Since only the master can open its mouth, there's no need for arbitration. Slaves can even drop their crystal and re-sync their clock from the Sync byte every frame, which is exactly why LIN is cheap.

The usual topology is CAN as the backbone, LIN as the sub-networks hanging off it, bridged by a gateway.
